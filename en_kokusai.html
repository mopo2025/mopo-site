Sapporo

Sapporo Kokusai

Big Hokkaido snow, an easy day trip from Sapporo.

Less than an hour from central Sapporo, the city gives way to deep snow and forested mountain runs. Sapporo Kokusai is known for reliable powder, generous pistes and a long season, with terrain for first turns as well as confident skiers and snowboarders. Add a post-ski soak at nearby Jozankei Onsen and it becomes a complete winter day out—without changing your Sapporo base.

Resort overview

Seven runs cover a balanced mix of beginner (30%), intermediate (40%) and advanced (30%) terrain. The season typically stretches from mid-November into early May, making Kokusai a strong choice at both ends of a Hokkaido ski holiday.

Newcomers can build confidence on broad, gentle slopes before linking into a 3.6-kilometre cruise. More experienced riders can seek out a 30-degree downhill run, moguls and ungroomed powder areas shaped by the resort’s abundant snowfall.

Kokusai works especially well for travellers staying in Sapporo: head up for a full day on snow, unwind in Jozankei Onsen afterwards, then return to the city for dinner. It is a simple way to combine mountain time with the food, shopping and nightlife of an urban holiday.

Which runs suit your level?

Kokusai is compact enough to navigate easily, while still offering a clear progression from first turns to steeper terrain. Start with the terrain that matches your group and adjust to the day’s visibility and snow conditions.

First-time skiers and riders

Broad, gentle slopes provide room to practise stopping, turning and using the lifts before moving onto longer green runs.

Long cruising laps

The Woody and Märchen runs can be linked for around 3.6 kilometres—ideal once you can control speed and connect turns comfortably.

Powder and natural terrain

After fresh snowfall, designated ungroomed areas offer softer, deeper turns. Follow resort signs and never enter closed or out-of-bounds areas.

A stronger challenge

The 30-degree Downhill course and mogul terrain suit experienced skiers and snowboarders with reliable turn and speed control.

Summit views, snow play and mountain food

The Sky Cabin climbs towards the 1,100-metre summit area, where clear days can reveal views across Ishikari Bay. In the right cold and humid conditions, snow and ice coat the trees to create juhyo, or “snow monsters”. Non-skiing companions can also take a return gondola ride for the scenery.

A snow-play area near the base gives families another way to enjoy the mountain. The resort has several restaurants in the centre house and a summit café; local favourites highlighted by the tourism office include Kokusai curry ramen, freshly baked pizza and soft serve.

Skis, snowboards, boots and outerwear are available to rent, making this a practical day trip for travellers from Singapore or Australia who prefer to arrive without bulky equipment.

Pair your ski day with Jozankei Onsen

Jozankei is a hot-spring town set in a forested gorge within Shikotsu-Toya National Park, about 30 minutes by car from Sapporo Kokusai. In winter, open-air baths look onto snow-covered trees—an easy way to turn a day on the slopes into a distinctly Hokkaido experience.

Open for stay and itinerary ideas

Day trip: Ski during the day, visit a facility offering day-use bathing, then continue to Sapporo. Bathing hours and closing days vary, so check directly before setting out.

Stay overnight: An onsen stay gives you more time for snowy gorge walks, footbaths and a slower evening. The ski resort is around 30 minutes away by car, making Jozankei useful for consecutive snow days.

Midwinter atmosphere: The valley takes on an ink-painting quality under deep snow. Seasonal events such as the Snow Candle Way may also be held; dates change each year.

by train & bus

✦ Direct shuttle buses
Direct buses operate from Sapporo Station and major city hotels. Services require advance reservation, and package options are available that include both a lift ticket and round-trip transport.

Click to enlarge

✦ Jozankei Onsen connection
From Jozankei Onsen, local buses also connect directly to the resort. Many visitors combine a ski day with a relaxing soak in the hot springs—a classic “ski & onsen” experience.

by car

The resort has 1,850 free parking spaces. Mountain roads may be snowy, icy or affected by poor visibility, so use a properly winter-equipped vehicle and allow extra time. If you are not experienced in winter driving, the reserved bus is the safer option.

  • About 100 minutes from New Chitose Airport
  • About 60 minutes from central Sapporo
  • About 40 minutes from central Otaru
  • About 20 minutes from Asari IC
  • About 30 minutes from Jozankei Onsen

On the Otaru route, part of Hokkaido Route 1 is normally closed overnight from 7 pm to 7 am during winter. Weather and road conditions can add considerable time, so check current notices before departure.

Equipment rental—our recommendation

We recommend that students rent directly from Sapporo Kokusai.

The resort offers ski and snowboard sets, boots and snow clothing. Online booking is available; reserving ahead and keeping the issued QR code ready can shorten the collection process on arrival.
